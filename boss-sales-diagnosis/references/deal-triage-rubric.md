# Deal Triage Rubric

Use this to decide whether the recommendation should be Invest, Repair, Nurture, or Stop.

## Four Decisions

### Invest

Choose Invest when:
- The customer has real business pain and urgency.
- The seller can reach or influence the real decision chain.
- The deal has strong margin, strategic value, reference value, or repeat potential.
- Extra effort creates durable trust or a reusable case, not just one discounted order.

Typical moves:
- Owner joins one key meeting.
- Build a tailored one-page business case.
- Offer phased pilot with explicit success criteria.
- Trade extra resources for access, timeline, or commitment.

Do not Invest when the customer only wants free consulting, endless comparison, or one-sided concessions.

### Repair

Choose Repair when:
- The deal is not dead, but the seller has made a process or value-translation mistake.
- The customer has gone silent after proposal, demo, quote, or objection.
- The contact may still be useful but lacks internal explanation material.
- One high-quality reframe can reveal whether there is real opportunity.

Typical moves:
- Send a concise reframe message.
- Provide a one-page value/risk comparison.
- Ask for a 15-minute alignment call.
- Set a clear next-step proposal and a soft exit.

Repair means one or two deliberate moves, not indefinite chasing.

### Nurture

Choose Nurture when:
- Timing is wrong, budget is not ready, or internal consensus is immature.
- The customer is valuable but cannot move now.
- Continuing to push would damage posture or trust.
- The seller can maintain low-frequency, high-value contact.

Typical moves:
- Stop asking "how is the decision going".
- Send occasional insight tied to the customer's business.
- Reopen only when a trigger appears: budget, leadership change, risk event, regulation, expansion, failure of current vendor.

### Stop

Choose Stop when:
- The customer has poor fit, low margin, high delivery risk, or values conflict.
- The customer wants premium responsibility at low-price conditions.
- The decision chain is inaccessible and the contact has no internal influence.
- Every new step asks the seller to invest while the customer gives no commitment.

Typical moves:
- Exit respectfully.
- Leave a future door open if conditions change.
- Protect resources for better-fit opportunities.

## Fast Scoring

Score each item 0-2.

```text
Pain / urgency:
Decision-chain access:
Value fit:
Margin / strategic value:
Customer cooperation:
Delivery risk control:
```

Guide:
- 10-12: Invest
- 7-9: Repair or Invest, depending on strategic value
- 4-6: Nurture or one limited Repair
- 0-3: Stop

Override rules:
- If delivery risk is uncontrolled and margin is low, downgrade to Stop.
- If decision-chain access is zero, do not Invest.
- If strategic value is high but current timing is weak, choose Nurture.
- If the seller caused confusion but customer pain is real, choose Repair.
