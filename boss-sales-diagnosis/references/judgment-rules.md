# Judgment Rules

These rules capture owner/operator B2B sales judgment. Use them to make diagnoses sharper. Do not dump rule names into the answer; apply the judgment naturally.

## 1. Quote Is Never Just Price

Trigger:
- customer asks for a quote
- seller wants to send a number quickly
- proposal has only price and product list

Judgment:
A B2B quote is a rare written communication moment. If it only states price, it wastes a trust-building chance.

Wrong move:
- send a bare price
- attach a product sheet and wait

Better move:
- include thanks, background understanding, customer issue, proposed path, cases, assumptions, and price

Diagnostic question:
"这份报价除了告诉客户多少钱，还证明了你理解他什么？"

## 2. Written Courtesy Is Sales Competence

Trigger:
- proposal, quotation, follow-up email, WeChat summary
- user asks how to appear professional

Judgment:
Courtesy is not decoration. In long B2B cycles, written courtesy proves discipline, respect, and seriousness.

Wrong move:
- casual files, rough formatting, unclear structure
- "客户只看价格" as an excuse for poor documents

Better move:
- use a clean structure, clear headings, concise thanks, and a professional record of prior discussion

Diagnostic question:
"客户转发你的文件给老板时，会觉得你专业，还是觉得你随便？"

## 3. Do Not Worship Sales Scripts

Trigger:
- user asks for magic words
- user focuses on closing tricks
- problem involves complex B2B or high-value services

Judgment:
Scripts can polish expression, but they cannot replace truth, trust, and diagnosis. In complex B2B, overusing tricks usually damages posture.

Wrong move:
- force urgency
- use manipulative wording
- copy viral "sales champion" scripts

Better move:
- first diagnose what truth has not been found, then write a plain message that reveals or advances that truth

Diagnostic question:
"你现在缺一句话，还是缺一个真实判断？"

## 4. Pressure Should Become Stage Goals

Trigger:
- seller anxiety
- long sales cycle
- user asks how to push faster

Judgment:
Large deals create pressure. If the only success marker is signing, the seller will deform their actions. Stage goals reduce anxiety and improve behavior.

Wrong move:
- chase signatures every interaction
- call too often because of internal pressure

Better move:
- set small goals: earn a meeting, learn a hidden concern, become preferred option, get internal introduction

Diagnostic question:
"除了签约，这次互动要让客户多信你哪一分？"

## 5. Relationship Dependency Is Fragile

Trigger:
- deal relies on one leader, one contact, or one big client
- user says relationship is strong
- business depends on guanxi

Judgment:
Relationship can help, but single-point dependency is fragile. People change, power shifts, and favors expire.

Wrong move:
- treat relationship as moat
- ignore value proof and multi-point connection

Better move:
- turn relationship into broader organizational trust and visible value

Diagnostic question:
"如果这个关键人明天离职，这单还剩下什么？"

## 6. Boss Anxiety Is Often Hidden Demand

Trigger:
- customer is owner/founder
- customer is irritable, hesitant, controlling, or risk-averse
- business model is fragile

Judgment:
Many bosses are not buying products; they are buying safety against business fragility.

Wrong move:
- respond only to surface requirements
- treat anger as personality problem

Better move:
- identify what insecurity the solution reduces: unstable orders, hard collection, low efficiency, weak team, technical bottleneck, customer concentration

Diagnostic question:
"这个老板最怕的不是贵，而是什么失控？"

## 7. Sales Is Translation Between Two Professional Worlds

Trigger:
- technical team says customer does not understand
- customer cannot grasp professional details
- seller hides behind "I do not understand technology"

Judgment:
The seller's value is translating professional supply into customer-understandable business value.

Wrong move:
- let technicians speak jargon directly
- avoid technical content entirely

Better move:
- translate technology into cost, reliability, risk, speed, compliance, safety, or business outcome

Diagnostic question:
"客户听完以后知道技术更厉害，还是知道自己的问题更安全了？"

## 8. Customer Needs Are Also Professional

Trigger:
- professionals dismiss customer as ignorant
- customer requirement looks naive
- seller feels customer "does not know what they need"

Judgment:
Understanding customer needs, especially hidden needs, is a professional skill equal to technical expertise.

Wrong move:
- mock customer requirements
- educate with superiority

Better move:
- ask what business condition produced the requirement and what risk the customer is trying to avoid

Diagnostic question:
"这个看似外行的要求，背后在保护什么？"

## 9. Output Understanding, Not Product

Trigger:
- proposal has no response
- customer keeps giving feedback
- seller talks mostly about product

Judgment:
When you output product, the outcome is win or lose. When you output understanding, every customer response improves your understanding.

Wrong move:
- defend product features
- treat feedback as objection

Better move:
- show your current understanding and invite correction

Diagnostic question:
"你发给客户的是产品说明，还是对他处境的理解？"

## 10. A Good Solution Requires Restraint

Trigger:
- seller wants to over-design
- proposal is impressive but costly
- user wants to maximize their own side

Judgment:
Business restraint is wisdom. A strong solution balances need, cost, personalization, and mutual gain.

Wrong move:
- recommend high configuration to look brilliant
- maximize seller benefit and call it win-win

Better move:
- design the highest-value path within the customer's acceptance and implementation capacity

Diagnostic question:
"这个方案是在帮客户赢，还是在展示你自己？"

## 11. Confidence Comes From Understanding

Trigger:
- seller lacks confidence
- seller overtalks, pushes, or uses pressure
- customer seems uneasy

Judgment:
Real confidence comes from deep understanding of product, customer need, and mutual value. Forced persuasion reveals weak confidence.

Wrong move:
- talk harder to compensate
- pressure customer into agreement

Better move:
- deepen diagnosis until the recommendation becomes naturally firm

Diagnostic question:
"你敢不敢不催客户，也相信自己的方案站得住？"

## 12. Go To The Scene

Trigger:
- remote discussion is stuck
- team cannot understand the problem
- customer situation is operational or physical

Judgment:
The scene contains clues that chat, calls, and meetings cannot provide. Some sales insights only appear on site.

Wrong move:
- keep discussing abstractions online
- let files replace observation

Better move:
- visit, observe, test, simulate, and infer customer thinking from real conditions

Diagnostic question:
"你现在是在想象客户现场，还是已经看过现场？"

## 13. Do Not Confuse Character Trust With Capability Trust

Trigger:
- user says customer trusts them
- relationship is friendly but project fails
- old employee or loyal person is assigned to new task

Judgment:
Being trusted as a person does not prove one can solve the problem. In business, capability trust usually comes first.

Wrong move:
- rely on loyalty or friendliness
- assume personal trust can replace proof

Better move:
- explicitly prove capability, process, and risk control

Diagnostic question:
"客户是相信你这个人，还是相信你能把结果交出来？"

## 14. Maximum Win-Win Means Helping Customer Use Value Fully

Trigger:
- seller defines success as closing
- product is suitable but usage/implementation value is unclear
- customer underuses the solution

Judgment:
Selling the right thing is not enough. Maximum win-win includes helping the customer extract the full value after purchase.

Wrong move:
- stop caring after signature
- sell only what fits now

Better move:
- plan adoption, implementation, training, follow-up, and value realization

Diagnostic question:
"客户买完以后，能不能真的把价值用出来？"

## 15. Become The Customer's First Choice

Trigger:
- seller is anxious about close date
- timing is uncertain
- long sales cycle

Judgment:
Signing time is often outside the seller's control. Becoming the customer's preferred option is more controllable and healthier.

Wrong move:
- measure every week by contract progress only

Better move:
- build preference through trust, understanding, useful work, and unique value

Diagnostic question:
"如果客户今天必须选一家，你是不是首选？如果不是，差在哪里？"

## 16. Product Comparison Should Become Solution Comparison

Trigger:
- customer compares vendors
- competitor has lower price
- product has no obvious differentiation

Judgment:
If comparison remains at product level, price dominates. The seller must move comparison to solution, risk, implementation, and hidden needs.

Wrong move:
- explain why our product is better in isolation
- attack competitor quality vaguely

Better move:
- define decision criteria around customer outcomes and total risk

Diagnostic question:
"客户现在是在比产品，还是在比谁能让他更安全地达成结果？"

## 17. "Price Sensitive" Is Often Underdiagnosed Need

Trigger:
- customer is labeled price-sensitive
- customer keeps asking for discount
- seller assumes customer has no budget

Judgment:
Customers can move from price-sensitive to value-sensitive when hidden needs are deeply understood.

Wrong move:
- categorize customer too early
- concede before value is clear

Better move:
- discover what "appropriate quality and price" means in their risk context

Diagnostic question:
"客户嫌贵，是因为没钱，还是因为没有看见贵在哪里值得？"

## 18. Decision-Maker Hidden Needs Matter

Trigger:
- proposal satisfies organizational need but stalls
- middle manager hesitates
- final approver is absent

Judgment:
Decision-makers have personal needs: career credit, reduced blame, internal justification, reliable partners. Ignoring them makes good proposals stall.

Wrong move:
- solve only the organization's stated need

Better move:
- help the decision-maker explain, defend, and safely own the decision

Diagnostic question:
"这个人推动你，会得到什么好处，又承担什么风险？"

## 19. Solution Is The Seller's Talent Made Visible

Trigger:
- product is standard
- market is crowded
- seller asks how to differentiate

Judgment:
In mature markets, the seller's unique understanding, resources, taste, and integration ability become part of the solution.

Wrong move:
- wait for product uniqueness
- compete only on price and features

Better move:
- add customer-meaningful uniqueness through design, process, service, risk control, or insight

Diagnostic question:
"同样的产品，经你之手，对客户多了什么独特价值？"

## 20. Demand Granularity Creates Uniqueness

Trigger:
- service looks commoditized
- customer seems to need only A
- seller cannot find differentiation

Judgment:
Look at customer demand in smaller grains. A can become A1, A2, A3, and hidden value may emerge at finer granularity.

Wrong move:
- accept broad demand labels

Better move:
- subdivide workflow, user, risk, timing, acceptance standard, and decision concerns

Diagnostic question:
"客户说要 A，但 A 里面哪一小块最让他不安？"

## 21. Do Not Chase Friendship As A Sales Method

Trigger:
- user wants to "be friends first"
- customer relation is warm but vague
- entertainment replaces business value

Judgment:
Friendship may result from trust, but it is not the method. First display unique value; the relationship category will follow.

Wrong move:
- use private closeness to compensate for weak value

Better move:
- let customers choose you because your value and posture are clear

Diagnostic question:
"如果去掉吃喝闲聊，客户还为什么选你？"

## 22. Sales Is Two-Way Selection

Trigger:
- customer is hostile, exploitative, or misaligned
- seller feels forced to accept unfair terms
- user asks whether to keep chasing

Judgment:
B2B sales is not begging. It is mutual selection. Bad-fit customers can damage morale, margin, and delivery.

Wrong move:
- accept any customer for revenue

Better move:
- evaluate customer quality and stop early when values or economics are wrong

Diagnostic question:
"这单赢下来，是资产，还是债务？"

## 23. Hard Strength Opens Doors; Soft Strength Builds Connection

Trigger:
- small company competes with large company
- user lacks scale, brand, or cases
- customer initially favors big vendor

Judgment:
Customers first see hard strength, but soft strength can build deeper connection over time.

Wrong move:
- imitate large vendors on their battlefield
- compete only by lowering price

Better move:
- use understanding, speed, flexibility, attention, and owner mentality to create connection

Diagnostic question:
"大公司有的硬实力你没有，那客户能从你身上得到什么软价值？"

## 24. "Cost-Performance" May Be Small Win-Win

Trigger:
- seller emphasizes high cost-performance
- customer only talks function and price
- product has hidden soft value

Judgment:
Cost-performance focuses on hard value. "Worth it" includes soft value and hidden need. High cost-performance may still be only minimal win-win.

Wrong move:
- reduce the sale to function divided by price

Better move:
- reveal why the choice is worth it in business, emotional safety, trust, and long-term use

Diagnostic question:
"客户觉得你便宜，还是觉得你值得？"

## 25. Expression Must Keep Returning To Need

Trigger:
- call, meeting, WeChat, proposal, or follow-up
- user asks how to communicate

Judgment:
Sales expression can be flexible, but the center must not drift: repeatedly show understanding of customer need and how it will be satisfied.

Wrong move:
- long chat that dilutes business intent
- WeChat voice messages for important points
- scattered messages with no structure

Better move:
- concise written summaries and need-centered oral communication

Diagnostic question:
"这次表达的神在哪里？客户能不能看出你一直围绕他的需求？"

## 26. Professional Means Understanding Each Role

Trigger:
- user says they are professional because they know the product
- deal has multiple stakeholders
- procurement, boss, user, and technical team differ

Judgment:
Sales professionalism is not only product knowledge. It is understanding the needs of each role and solving accordingly.

Wrong move:
- talk to every stakeholder with the same pitch

Better move:
- map what each role fears, wants, measures, and must explain

Diagnostic question:
"你对老板、采购、使用人、技术方的不同需求分别理解到哪一层？"

## 27. Survival Itself Builds Trust

Trigger:
- user has long operating history
- customer or old acquaintance returns after years
- company lacks flashy proof

Judgment:
In many traditional industries, a company still being alive after years is itself evidence of customer recognition and reliability.

Wrong move:
- underuse operating history

Better move:
- present longevity as proof of stability, fulfillment, and customer continuity

Diagnostic question:
"你活到今天，背后有哪些客户持续认可可以被说清楚？"

## 28. Compete With The Customer's Wrong View, Not Only Competitors

Trigger:
- customer has backward view, bias, or narrow understanding
- competitor discussion is distracting
- seller wants a bigger solution

Judgment:
The most valuable competition may be with the customer's outdated assumptions. Each time you help the customer see better, value rises.

Wrong move:
- only fight competitor

Better move:
- respectfully challenge the customer's false premise with evidence, scene, and small experiments

Diagnostic question:
"这局真正挡住你的，是同行，还是客户脑子里的旧判断？"

## 29. Good Products Should Reduce Sales Friction

Trigger:
- user blames sales for poor results
- product needs heavy explanation or costly marketing
- startup asks how to sell

Judgment:
Sales experience reveals product and business friction. A good product/service has already solved some problems before entering the market.

Wrong move:
- throw advertising money at every blockage

Better move:
- identify which step blocks customers from reaching, understanding, trusting, buying, or using the product

Diagnostic question:
"客户不买，是销售没努力，还是产品/服务本身到达客户的路太难？"

## 30. Sales Process Is Truth-Seeking

Trigger:
- any stuck deal
- customer says one thing but behaves differently
- seller is confused by delay

Judgment:
Sales is trust-building and truth-seeking. Every obstacle asks for the truth behind it.

Wrong move:
- take surface explanations literally
- push without discovering the real reason

Better move:
- design the next interaction to reveal one important truth

Diagnostic question:
"下一步动作能不能帮你知道这单真正卡在哪里？"
