# Common Scenarios

Use these patterns to classify the user's situation. A deal can match more than one scenario.

## 1. Customer Says Price Is Too High

Likely real issues:
- value has not been translated into business impact
- comparison object is product price, not total solution
- the contact cannot justify the price upward
- the decision-maker sees risk but not upside

Diagnosis focus:
- What does the price difference buy?
- What happens if the customer chooses the cheaper option?
- Who must defend the purchase internally?
- Is there a way to reframe from quote to solution?

Recommended moves:
- do not rush to discount
- ask whether the issue is hard budget, unclear ROI, or internal approval risk
- provide a one-page value/risk comparison
- trade any concession for term, scope, prepayment, reference, or expansion

## 2. Proposal Sent, Customer Goes Silent

Likely real issues:
- proposal did not hit the true concern
- internal disagreement emerged
- the contact lost control of the process
- priority changed
- competitor entered

Diagnosis focus:
- Did the proposal express understanding or just product/service?
- Was there a clear next step before sending it?
- Who else needs to approve?
- What new value can justify contact without desperation?

Recommended moves:
- stop repeated low-value chasing
- send one reflective reframe message
- offer an exit path for honest feedback
- add new value relevant to the customer's business
- set a stop-loss point after two high-quality attempts

## 3. Cannot Reach Decision-Maker

Likely real issues:
- contact lacks incentive to introduce upward
- contact fears losing control
- seller has not made the contact safer or more successful
- decision-maker language is not ready

Diagnosis focus:
- What personal win does the contact get by introducing you?
- What risk do they perceive?
- Can you help them brief upward instead of bypassing them?

Recommended moves:
- do not bypass too early
- co-create a short upward briefing
- ask what the boss would care about most
- let the contact keep credit and control

## 4. Customer Keeps Comparing Vendors

Likely real issues:
- the seller is trapped at product level
- solution uniqueness is unclear
- hidden needs have not been surfaced
- the customer is using vendors to educate themselves

Diagnosis focus:
- Are you selling what you have or what the customer needs?
- What gap exists between customer need and vendor product?
- Can you create a solution combination others have not offered?

Recommended moves:
- move comparison from product to solution
- define decision criteria with customer
- expose hidden cost of the wrong choice
- show what you understand that competitors missed

## 5. Internal Boss Refuses Resources

Likely real issues:
- boss sees cost, not strategic value
- risk is not controlled
- sales has not earned belief
- the opportunity is not actually worth special investment

Diagnosis focus:
- Is this order or a strategic entry point?
- What is the downside if resources are approved and the deal fails?
- Can investment be staged by customer milestones?

Recommended moves:
- prepare a one-page opportunity investment note
- include strategic value, risk controls, resource ask, and exit mechanism
- ask boss to choose between options, not approve a vague request

## 6. Customer Relationship Is Good But Deal Does Not Move

Likely real issues:
- relationship trust exists, but capability or business trust does not
- customer enjoys interaction but lacks urgency
- no internal business owner is carrying the project
- the seller mistakes friendliness for progress

Diagnosis focus:
- What has the customer done that costs them time, risk, or political capital?
- Is there a real business deadline?
- Who owns the pain internally?

Recommended moves:
- test urgency with a business-impact question
- ask for a concrete next commitment
- stop treating friendly conversation as pipeline progress

## 7. Product Negative News Or Delivery Risk

Likely real issues:
- trust can be lost if the seller hides or minimizes
- the customer is judging character as much as facts
- crisis can become trust-building if handled with maturity

Diagnosis focus:
- What must the customer know to feel safe?
- What have you learned from the issue?
- What protective action can you offer?

Recommended moves:
- be transparent without panic
- explain impact, boundary, fix, and prevention
- connect the lesson to customer safety

## 8. Unsure Whether To Keep Chasing

Likely real issues:
- seller is emotionally attached to sunk cost
- opportunity quality is low
- customer may not match values or capability
- continued chasing damages posture

Diagnosis focus:
- What has changed since the first contact?
- Is there evidence of customer investment?
- Does this customer improve the business if won?

Recommended moves:
- classify as invest, repair, nurture, or stop
- set a next-action deadline
- if nurturing, switch to low-frequency high-value contact
